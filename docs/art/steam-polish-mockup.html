1 · The short version

Five changes, one look

Place

The Contract Road

Every job has a backdrop: the Mudroad, the Drowned Marsh, the Crimson Keep. The place changes when the threats do, so you can see things getting worse.

UI kit

One inked kit

Every panel, bar, slot, card and button drawn in the same hand-inked style. No more flat Godot boxes next to inked frames.

Type

Bigger, fewer sizes

Keep your three fonts. Nothing on screen under 18 px at 1080p, five sizes in total. It reads on a Steam Deck.

Life

Weight on every hit

Fighters breathe, flinch, stand on the ground and fall. Heals burst, combos punch, bursts shake the screen. All shaders and particles, no new drawings.

Steam

A store kit

Key art, logo, the eight capsule images, screenshots and a trailer. The capsule is the most important image you'll make.

2 · Setting

Where the jobs happen

Today the fights happen nowhere. A dark red gradient sits behind the party, the fighters float above it with no ground or shadow, and every round looks the same. Steam players judge a game from screenshots in a few seconds, and a screenshot with no place in it reads as a prototype. The fix is to give the game a place without taking colour away from the meanings.

A · Recommended

The Contract Road, in acts

Pythio follows the work down one road. Each stretch between Barons is an act with its own backdrop, and the backdrop changes on the round when a new kind of threat arrives.

  • Fits the story: a washed-up healer taking whatever job is next down the road.
  • Fits escalating chaos: the marsh arrives with poison at round 6, so you can see the danger coming.
  • Cost: 4 backdrops (3 acts + the Keep), each in 3–4 layers. You can make them in ChatGPT.
B · Low-change

One road at night

One backdrop behind every round and a red-lit hall for the Baron. It costs about a third as much, and the game stops looking like a prototype.

  • Two backdrops in total.
  • Gives nothing to escalation: round 20 looks like round 2.
  • Easy to upgrade to A later; it uses the same slot.

Tone rule: the place is mood, never information. Backdrops stay grey, dim and low-contrast behind a vignette. They never use emerald, electric blue, gold, icy cyan or violet, and only the Keep uses a muted red. If a backdrop ever competes with a health bar, the backdrop loses. Nothing is renamed: spell, fighter and file names stay exactly as they are (Phase H is yours).

3 · Palette

Same meanings, fixed values

The colour language stays exactly as decided. What changes is that each colour gets one exact value used everywhere: today the greens, reds and golds drift from screen to screen. The neutrals are new: ink, bone and sepia replace the default Godot greys. Every text colour below passes WCAG AA (4.5:1) on all three game backgrounds.

4 · Type, icons and the UI kit

Readable from the couch

Your three fonts already fit: Pirata One for titles, Oswald for the interface, Special Elite for Pythio. The problem is size. Today labels like "LIGHT ARMOR" and the battlefield rules are around 10–12 px at 1080p, and they shrink further on a Steam Deck. Valve's Deck checklist wants text at least 9 px tall at 1280×800. A floor of 18 px at 1080p gives 12 px on the Deck.

Icon rules

Square, dark tile, a rough double inked frame in bone, faint crosshatch inside, and one symbol glowing in its meaning colour with a black outline. Readable at 64 px. No text, no second colour unless it's part of a combo (Spirit Surge carries a blue line because it feeds on shields). These five are drawn as vector starters you can use as references in ChatGPT.

The UI kit (one set of pieces)

6 · Technology

Stay in Godot 2D

Nothing here needs a new engine or 3D. It's Godot 4.3 doing things it's good at: themes, shaders, particles and its new Parallax2D node for layered backdrops. The web build keeps working.

Theme and UI kitnewOne pythio_theme.tres using the 9-slice images instead of flat boxes
Feel layernewShaders on the fighter sprite (breathing, hit flash, ink-dissolve death), particles, screen shake, all driven by EventBus
Backdrop per actnew3–4 image layers in a Parallax2D, fog and rain particles, one scene per place
Game systemsunchangedGameManager, EffectSystem, DraftSystem and the rest: no rule changes
How fighters come aliveVerdict
Shaders and tweens on the existing images (breathing, flinch, hit flash, fall)Recommended. Works with every image you've already made, costs no art.
Cut-out rigs (Godot Skeleton2D: limbs as separate pieces)Later, maybe for Pythio only. Each character needs to be redrawn in parts.
Frame-by-frame animation from AI imagesNo. Frames don't match each other; it flickers.
3DNo. It throws away the inked look, which is the game's best asset.

Screens, sizes and budgets

Any screen shape

Switch the window stretch to canvas_items with expand. Today a Steam Deck (16:10) and ultrawide monitors get black bars; with expand, the backdrop fills the extra space and the HUD pins to the edges.

Sharp at 1440p and 4K

Characters at 1024 px tall (today 512), backdrop layers at 3840 × 2160. The web build can ship half-size copies.

Frame rate

60 fps on a Steam Deck with 8 fighters and a Baron fight. Under 300 particles on screen, under 512 MB of textures, under 500 MB download.

7 · Making the art

Same workflow, bigger pieces

Keep the workflow that made the characters: you make images in ChatGPT, send them to me with the file name, I cut them out, place them, screenshot them and open a PR.

Now · you + ChatGPT

Backdrops (one layer per request), the UI kit pieces, tag and spell icons, the knight and mercenary fallen poses.

Upscaling

ChatGPT tops out around 1536 px. Run backdrops and characters through a free upscaler such as Upscayl (runs on your PC) to reach 4K and 1024 px.

Hire for the capsule

The key art and capsule decide whether people click. A human illustrator, working from your characters, is the best-spent money on the whole store page.

Making one backdrop (Act I)

  1. Open ChatGPT, attach characters/knight/knight_1.png and the concept sheet as style references.
  2. Paste the "far layer" prompt below. Landscape, 3:2. Repeat until the horizon sits about a third of the way down.
  3. Do the same for the mid layer (props on a plain white background) and the ground (a road strip seen at a low angle).
  4. Upscale each layer 2.5× in Upscayl, then send them to me as backdrops/mudroad/far.png, mid.png, ground.png.
  5. I cut out the mid layer, set up the Parallax2D scene and send you a screenshot and a build to try.

Steam and AI images. Steam allows AI-generated art, but its content survey asks you to say so, and the answer shows on your store page. Check that the tool's terms give you commercial rights (ChatGPT's currently do). A hand-made capsule and key art also avoid some players' reaction to AI art on the store front.

10 · Plan

In this order

Each step ships on its own and the game looks better after every one, so you can stop at any point.

  1. Fix and resize (no new art). 18 px floor, the type scale, ink/bone colours, the overlapping titles on the pick and Next party screens, stretch to any screen shape, portrait removed.
  2. The feel layer (no new art). Ground shadows, breathing, hit flash, screen shake, heal bursts, combo punch, death fade. Everything in the mock-up above.
  3. The UI kit. You make the six pieces; I swap the theme over.
  4. Act I backdrop and the Keep. The two places you see most.
  5. Icons. The 7 tag icons, then one per spell (already on your list).
  6. Marsh, Chapel, and characters at 1024 px.
  7. The store kit. Key art and capsules, screenshots, trailer, icons.

What I need from you

  1. Setting: the Contract Road in acts (A, my pick) or one road at night (B)?
  2. Act names: Mudroad, Drowned Marsh, Plague Chapel, Crimson Keep. Keep them, or will you name them in Phase H?
  3. Portrait: can the bottom-right portrait go? Pythio is already on screen.
  4. Fonts: keep your three and add the 18 px floor (my pick), or reopen fonts now?
  5. Screen shape: switch to fill any screen shape, which fixes black bars on Steam Deck and ultrawide?
  6. Capsule and key art: make them yourself in ChatGPT, or budget for an illustrator (my pick)?
  7. Start: shall I start on steps 1 and 2 now? They need no art from you.

Risks: art is the slow part, and backdrops are the biggest images you've made so far. Screen shake and flashes need a "reduce effects" option in the pause menu for comfort and accessibility; I'll add one with step 2.
